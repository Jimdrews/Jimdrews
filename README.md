# James Andrews

### I teach machines to do paperwork, then take them apart to see how they think.

---

My first real code was for a solar car team: timing and analysis scripts to figure out why a car powered by sunlight was slower on Tuesdays. I've been chasing that same question ever since. *Why does this system do what it does, and how do I make it do it better?*

**By day** I build AI that does the work nobody wants to do: reading the invoice pile, routing every question to the right model, answering the customer who texts at 11pm. The demo is the easy part. Most of my job is making the thing still behave on its thousandth Monday: catching problems before customers do, keeping the codebase honest, and helping teammates ship good work.

**By night** I can't leave the black box alone. Using AI wasn't enough; I wanted to know what was happening inside it. So:

- **[DadGPT](https://github.com/Jimdrews/DadGPT):** I built a language model from nothing, just to see what "thinking" really looks like at 2am. (Mostly: bugs.) It talks now. *It's getting there.*
- **[forgeant](https://github.com/Jimdrews/forgeant):** A toolkit for building AI agents, for people who like knowing exactly what their software is doing. → [forgeant.dev](https://forgeant.dev)
- **[host-your-own-llm](https://github.com/Jimdrews/host-your-own-llm):** One command to put a model on your own hardware, because renting someone else's brain gets old.
- **[fastmcp-trello](https://github.com/Jimdrews/fastmcp-trello):** I gave my AI assistant the keys to my to-do board. It's more organized than I am now.

---

**What's next:** I'm open to new roles in AI engineering and LLM infrastructure, especially ones that let me do both halves: shipping agents people rely on, and understanding the machinery underneath.
